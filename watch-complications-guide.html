Watch Complications Explained

A complication is anything a watch does beyond telling the hours, minutes and seconds. Here is what the most common ones show, and how to read them.

  • Read6 min
  • RevisedNov 2026
  • LevelBeginner
FIG. 01DATE & DAY-DATESHEET 02

Date & day-date

A date ring under the dial is advanced once every 24 hours by a wheel driven from the hour wheel. A day-date adds a second disc for the weekday. Avoid setting the date by hand when the hands are between about 9 pm and 3 am, when the change mechanism may already be engaged.

FIG. 02GMT & DUAL TIMESHEET 02

GMT & dual time

A GMT watch adds a fourth hand that circles the dial once every 24 hours. Read it against a 24-hour bezel or chapter ring to follow a second time zone at a glance. Some designs let you jump the local hour hand on its own when you travel.

FIG. 03CHRONOGRAPHSHEET 02

Chronograph

A chronograph is a stopwatch built into the watch, run by its own clutch and wheels.

  • The top pusher starts and stops timing.
  • The bottom pusher resets the hands to zero.
  • Small sub-dials count elapsed minutes and hours.
FIG. 04MOON PHASESHEET 02

Moon phase

A painted moon disc, usually with 59 or 135 teeth, shows the moon waxing and waning through a shaped window. A 59-tooth disc drifts by about a day every two and a half years; finer gearing can stay correct for more than a century.

FIG. 05POWER RESERVE & CALENDARSSHEET 02

Power reserve & calendars

A power reserve indicator shows how much energy is left in the mainspring, so you know when to wind. An annual calendar handles months of 30 and 31 days and needs correcting once a year, at the end of February. A perpetual calendar also allows for leap years.
